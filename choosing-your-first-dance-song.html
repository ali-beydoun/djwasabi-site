Wedding music

How to choose your first dance song

A few things to try when you haven't found your song, or you've got too many favourites.

By Wassim, DJ Wasabi
30 October 2026

In this guide

Start with your music Try it together Choose the recording Plan the ending Before you decide

Start with the music you already share

I care about music fitting the people in front of me. Your first dance is a good place to start with that same idea.

Think about a song from a trip, an artist you both love, or something that always ends up playing in the car. You might simply enjoy hearing it together. That's enough to put it on the shortlist.

If you're starting from scratch, each pick a few songs and listen together. A familiar favourite is worth considering, even if you've heard it at other weddings.

Give the shortlist a living room test

Put a few possibilities on and move to them together. You don't need a routine. See whether you can hear the beat and find a pace that feels comfortable.

A song can sound beautiful through headphones and feel surprisingly tricky when you're dancing to it. If you're concentrating so hard on the next step that you've stopped enjoying it, try another.

If you're taking lessons, bring your shortlist to the teacher. Mention the shoes and clothes you'll be wearing, and how much room you'll have on the dance floor. Those details can affect what works.

Listen to the whole song, and pick the version

Listen beyond the chorus. Read the words so you know what the song says, then decide whether you're comfortable with it.

Try the original alongside any acoustic or instrumental versions you like. A different recording can change the pace, the introduction and the point where the vocals start.

Once you've chosen, save the exact link and share it with your DJ or band. Use that recording when you practise, especially if you're learning a routine.

Decide how you'd like it to finish

You might want the whole song. You might be happier dancing to a shorter section. Talk with your DJ or band about what's possible and where the music could finish naturally.

If you'd like guests to join you partway through, agree when that invitation should happen and who will make it. It gives everyone handling the music and announcements a clear cue.

Keep both of you in the conversation. A simple dance is fine if that's what you enjoy. If one of you is nervous about being alone on the floor, plan around that before the wedding.

Before you decide

  • Do we both enjoy this song?
  • Have we tried moving to it together?
  • Have we picked the exact recording?
  • Have we agreed how the dance will end?

Keep your shortlist handy for the music planning conversation. You don't need every answer before you get in touch.
