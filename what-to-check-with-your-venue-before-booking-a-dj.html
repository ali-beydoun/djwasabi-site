What should we check with our venue before booking a DJ?

Before you sign, I'd ask the venue when the music stops, when everyone leaves, where the DJ would set up and what happens if it rains.

By Wassim, DJ Wasabi
31 October 2026

In this guide

Two timesSound limitsAccessPower and spaceOutsideBefore you sign

If you've found a venue you love, I'd have a short conversation with them about music before you sign. When does the music have to stop, when does everyone have to leave, where would the DJ set up, and what happens if it rains? The answers change how the night runs.

Every venue sets its own conditions. Some come from a liquor licence or a council approval, others are house rules. I'd get them in writing early, so we can talk about how they suit the night you have in mind.

When does the music stop, and when do we leave?

I'd ask the venue for both times and write them down. The time between the last song and the door is where farewells, a final drink and packing up all have to fit. If those two times are the same, the end of your night will feel rushed.

Tell me the answer when we plan. Then we can place the moments that matter to you, such as speeches, cake or a farewell, well before the music has to come down.

Is there a sound limiter, and how does it work there?

Some venues monitor how loud the room gets, usually because of neighbours or a condition on their licence. The equipment varies. Some systems show a warning light when the level climbs. Others cut the power to the music if it stays too high for a few seconds, and it may need someone from the venue to reset it.

I'd ask which kind they have, what level it's set to, and who resets it on the night. If the venue you love has a limiter, we plan around it together: where the speakers sit, and how the room sounds once it's full of people singing. Guests singing along can set some systems off, which is worth knowing before the chorus of your favourite song.

When can the DJ get in, and when do we have to be out?

Tell me what time suppliers can arrive and how long after the finish everything has to be gone. Some venues include set-up and pack-down in the hire period; others treat it as extra time you need to arrange. The route in matters too: a loading dock, a lift, a flight of stairs or a long walk from the car park all change how the afternoon goes.

Find out who your contact at the venue will be on the night. When the timing shifts, it helps to know who can answer a question without interrupting you at your table.

Where's the power, and where will people be?

I'd ask where the power points are in relation to the dance floor, and whether the venue expects you to use its own sound system or an in-house technician. Tell me what they say, so we can discuss whether that arrangement suits what you're planning.

Ask where the DJ usually sets up and where the dancing happens. A booth tucked in a far corner changes how a room feels. If you can, send me a photo of the space, and we can talk through the layout before the day.

Will any of it happen outside?

If you're planning drinks on a terrace or a ceremony in the garden, I'd ask whether amplified music is allowed out there at all, and until what time. Some venues allow it only indoors. If they do allow it, ask where the power is and what cover there is.

Ask what the wet-weather plan is: which room, who makes the call, and when. If your ceremony is outside, I've written separately about planning the music for a ceremony, including how the celebrant is heard.

Before you sign

  • Do we have both times in writing: music off and everyone out?
  • Who is our contact on the night?
  • Who makes the wet-weather call, and when?

Bring the answers to our chat. 'Not sure yet' is a fine answer for now. The same questions apply to a birthday in a function room or a work party at a restaurant. If you're thinking about a party at home instead, the questions are different, and I've written about what I'd sort out first.

Further reading

Some venues publish their conditions. For example, Estate Vaucluse House states that amplified music is restricted to indoors, that a sound limiter is in place and that events finish by 10.30pm. The Sydney Opera House events kit (PDF) describes supplier access before an event and the time allowed to pack down afterwards. Licensed venues also have to follow the noise conditions on their licence and any local precinct rules, which the NSW Government's guidance on dealing with disturbances explains. Your venue's answers will be its own.

Found your venue?

Tell me what they said about finish times, sound and set-up, even if some answers are still coming. We can plan the music around your room.
