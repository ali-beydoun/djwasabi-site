For the ceremony, I'd ask about four moments: your guests arriving, walking in, signing, and walking out. Each one wants something a little different, and it's easier to talk about them one at a time.

You don't need songs chosen before we talk. Where the ceremony is, how long the walk is and who's involved are more useful at the start.

Music while your guests arrive

People start arriving well before the ceremony. Relaxed music while they find a seat is usually all that's needed, and it's a good place for favourites that don't suit the walk itself.

Tell me roughly how long guests will be waiting and whether you'd prefer instrumental versions here. If there's a song that means something to you but feels too big for the aisle, this is often where it fits.

How long is the walk?

Tell me how far you'll be walking and who's walking before you. A short aisle with two people needs a different amount of music from a long garden path with a whole wedding party. We can agree where in the song you'd like to arrive, and who will give the cue to start.

If you'd like one song for the wedding party and another for you, say so, and listen to the join between them. If you'd rather keep one song for everyone, that's simpler, and it works.

Allow for the signing

After the vows there's a pause while you sign with your witnesses, and often photos as well. It usually takes a few minutes, sometimes longer than you'd expect. One song may not cover it, so choose a second you'd be happy to hear, or let me know you'd prefer the first to simply continue.

Read the lyrics of anything you pick for this moment. The room is quiet while you sign, so the words carry.

Walking out

Most couples want something here that lifts the mood. Choose a song you both love and tell me whether you'd like it to keep playing while guests congratulate you, or whether the drinks music follows straight on.

I cover ceremonies with a separate setup from the reception. If your ceremony and reception are in different places, or in the same place with a change-around in between, tell me so we can talk through how the music moves from one to the other.

Can everyone hear?

Outdoors, wind or traffic can drown out a quiet voice. Ask your celebrant how they're amplified. Some bring their own microphone and speaker, and part of their job is making sure your guests can hear. Tell me what they said, so we can discuss whether the music and the celebrant's sound are working together or separately.

Ask about permission too. Churches and some venues restrict music or amplification, and some councils need to approve amplified sound in public spaces. Check before you fall in love with a song. If you're still choosing a venue, I've written about what to check about music before you sign.

Before our chat

  • How far is the walk, and who's walking?
  • What happens during the signing, and for how long?
  • Is any of it outside, and how is the celebrant amplified?
  • Does the venue or church restrict music or amplification?

Once you've chosen, send me the exact recordings, the same as I'd ask for your first dance. Versions vary, and the walk should be timed to the one you'll hear.
