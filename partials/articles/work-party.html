Tell me how well people know each other

A team that works together every day has a different starting point from staff meeting colleagues from another branch for the first time. Let me know if there are new starters coming, or partners who won't know many people.

I'd ask what the group enjoys outside work too. A few music suggestions from the team can help. Their job titles won't tell me which songs they love, or whether they'd enjoy a game on the microphone.

If there are office jokes you want included in an introduction, explain them beforehand. Think about whether everyone in the room will understand them, and whether the person involved would enjoy the attention.

Give people a chance to settle in

For the start of a staff dinner, I'd keep the music at a level where people can hear each other across the table. Show me where people will be sitting and where you'd like dancing to happen, so we can discuss the setup with the venue.

Leave some time for arrivals before the welcome or first speech. If part of the team is coming straight from a shift, factor that in. You don't want their first few minutes to be spent hurrying through the room while someone is speaking.

Agree what happens on the microphone

If I'm also MCing, tell me how much involvement you want from the room. A short welcome and introductions may be enough. If you're considering games, talk through what people would be asked to do before you commit to them.

I'd keep participation optional and avoid calling someone out for being quiet. You can invite people to join in without handing a microphone to a person who wasn't expecting it.

For awards or thank-yous, check names and pronunciations, and decide whether recipients need to say anything. If you want someone to give a speech, ask them beforehand. That gives them time to prepare and a chance to tell you they're uncomfortable.

Make it clear when the speeches are finished

Agree with the host when the formal part ends. A brief announcement can let people know they're free to move around, with a change in music as the evening moves towards dancing.

Check that timing with the venue too. If food is about to arrive, I'd give people time to eat. Tell me if some guests need to leave early, so we can discuss when to include the moments you'd like them there for.

What if the dance floor stays quiet?

I'd look at the rest of the room before deciding the music needs a big change. People talking and enjoying themselves at their tables may be having exactly the night they wanted.

If dancing is something you're hoping for, say so when we plan. We can talk about music the team enjoys and leave time for it after dinner. If people are happy where they are, I'd leave them to enjoy themselves without repeated calls to get up.
