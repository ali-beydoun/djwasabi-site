A friend or family member can be a lovely choice to MC your wedding if they want the job and you both feel comfortable with them hosting. Hiring a professional is worth considering if you'd feel more relaxed handing the announcements and timing to someone outside your guest list.

I'd talk about that before choosing anyone. Would you enjoy hearing someone close to you introduce the speeches? Or would you spend dinner worrying about whether they know what happens next? Your comfort as a couple matters, along with theirs.

Would a friend enjoy being your MC?

Someone who knows you can bring warmth to the introductions without needing your whole story explained. You may also save the cost of hiring an MC. If you already have a willing person in mind, there's no reason to assume you need to pay someone else.

Give them a chance to understand the job before they answer. Being funny over dinner doesn't tell you whether they'd enjoy speaking to the whole room, preparing names and checking when the next part of the reception is ready.

They'll still be a guest, but there will be moments when they need to leave a conversation or check with the venue. Ask whether they'd be happy doing that. Make it easy to say no, especially if they're also giving a speech or have another role in the wedding.

What would you be paying a professional to do?

A professional MC gives you someone whose job is to host, so your friends and family can stay involved as guests. That may be worth the expense if neither of you wants to ask someone you know, or if the announcements are something you'd otherwise keep worrying about.

There is an extra cost, and you need to feel comfortable with their style. Ask how they would introduce you and your speakers. Talk about humour and anything you'd prefer left out. A polished delivery won't suit you if the personality feels wrong for your wedding.

Ask what the price covers too. Will they check timing with the venue, find the next speaker and coordinate music cues with the DJ? Who handles changes? Compare the actual responsibilities, including any MC option already available with your DJ, before deciding whether another booking is worth it.

Who would make you feel comfortable in the room?

If you're worried about awkward jokes or being made the centre of attention, say so before you choose. You can ask for brief introductions, agree which stories are off limits and decide whether extra speeches are welcome. Have that conversation with a friend just as you would with a professional.

Think about your guests too. Would the person you're considering be comfortable addressing everyone, including people they haven't met? Can you tell them when an idea doesn't suit you? You should feel able to ask for the kind of reception you want.

What should you agree once you've chosen?

Keep the preparation manageable. Give your MC the names and pronunciations, the agreed running order and a venue contact for timing. Your DJ needs the same current plan, including who gives each music cue.

Arrange a microphone check with whoever is handling the sound. Agree who finds speakers and passes on changes, so those jobs don't fall back to you during dinner.
