If you're wondering how your friends' music will fit alongside your family's favourites, send me some songs you love. You don't need to plan every song for the party. I'd start with the ones that matter most to you, then talk about who's coming and what they enjoy.

A shared playlist is fine too. If everyone has added something, you don't have to sort it into a running order. A few notes can help me understand which songs matter to you and where you're happy for me to choose.

Which songs would you miss?

Go through the list and pick out the songs you'd be disappointed to leave out. Tell me why they matter, especially if you want one for a particular moment.

For example, you might want a favourite playing when the birthday person arrives. That's different from a song you'd be happy to hear at some point while people are dancing. I'd want to talk through the timing with you before the party.

Include the artist and the version you mean. A remix or a live recording can sound quite different from the one you had in mind. A link helps identify it so we can check we're talking about the same track.

What do the other choices tell me?

The rest of your list can show me what you and your guests enjoy. You might have added several songs because you like that artist, or because they remind you of nights out with your friends. Say so. It helps me understand which details you're attached to and where you're happy for me to choose.

If you've got relatives and friends of different ages coming, tell me what they enjoy too. I'd ask about music you share: songs played at family gatherings, for instance, or something you and your parents both love. Someone's age alone doesn't tell me their taste.

You can also mention when people are likely to leave. If your grandparents are heading home after the cake, that's useful context for our music chat. We can discuss where their favourites might fit.

Are there songs you want left out?

A do-not-play list is welcome. You don't need to explain a personal reason for avoiding a song. Just make the instruction clear.

Be specific about whether you mean one track, an artist or a whole style of music. If you're unsure, we can talk it through. A broad instruction such as 'no old music' could rule out a family favourite you'd still like included.

For younger guests, tell me about any restrictions on lyrics. If a particular song matters, we should discuss the version before the night. A clean edit may still have themes you'd prefer to avoid.

How should requests work on the night?

I'm happy to take requests. Before the party, tell me how much room you'd like to leave for them and whether anyone else will be helping you make music decisions.

A request may suit a later part of the evening, when more people are ready to dance. I'll also be watching how people respond, as I explain in My Story. We should discuss any important requests ahead of time so we can check them and agree what you're expecting.

When you send your list, add a short note: which songs matter most, which are examples of your taste, what to avoid, and anything I should know about the guests or the timing. Bring any questions to our birthday music chat too.
